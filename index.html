Psychophysics & Computer Vision Study Strictly Anonymous Protocol • ~8–10 Minutes

Blur and Scrambling: People vs. Vision Models

Research Team: Uriel-Darlin Azemfack-Himele, Fanès Houeha, Halik Mamam, and Franck Tchangani.

1. Research Question & Scientific Background

This study investigates a fundamental question in biological and artificial vision: Which types of visual degradation break human recognition and modern Vision Transformers together, and which break only one of them?

We compare human observers against two state-of-the-art pretrained Vision Transformer models—CLIP ViT-L/14 and DINOv2 ViT-B/14—when classifying brief grayscale line drawings from the Google Quick, Draw! dataset across 10 everyday object categories.

2. Visual Degradations Tested

  • Intact Baseline: Uncorrupted black-on-white line drawings (`224×224` resolution).
  • Gaussian Blur ($\sigma \in \{2, 5, 10\}$ px): Progressively removes fine high-frequency stroke edges while preserving coarse spatial layout.
  • Offset Tile Scrambling ($4, 16, 64$ tiles): Cuts the drawing into a grid shifted by $+7\text{ px}$ (half of a $14\text{ px}$ ViT patch) and randomly permutes all tiles so local stroke parts remain visible while global object configuration is disrupted.

3. Experimental Procedure (70 Trials)

You will first complete 2 intact warm-up trials, followed by 70 main trials. On every trial:

  1. A central fixation cross (`+`) appears for 0.6 seconds.
  2. A line drawing is displayed for exactly 1.5 seconds.
  3. A brief visual noise mask appears for 0.4 seconds to clear retinal afterimages.
  4. You select which of the 10 categories matches the drawing (within 15 seconds; any unanswered drawing is automatically re-queued at the end of your session).

4. Strict Anonymity & Data Protection

No personal name, email, or identifying information is collected. You are automatically assigned an anonymous Participant Number (P01–P21, organized into 3 balanced groups of 7 participants covering all 490 experimental stimuli).

At the end of the 70 trials, your anonymous category responses and reaction times are automatically transmitted to the research database.
